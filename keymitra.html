01 / FIELD RESEARCH

Small workspaces.
Everyday friction.

We began by observing roadside keymakers’ work environments and workflows, supported by conversations with around 30 keymakers across Mumbai.

What we found

  • Limited space: keymakers worked in compact roadside workspaces.
  • Hard-to-access materials: tools and key blanks were not always organised.
  • Repetitive searching: finding tools and materials introduced repeated movements during work.
  • Manual customer records: paper-based details took time to store and retrieve.

These observations revealed two related design opportunities: organising physical workspaces, and organising information without adding physical storage.

Our “How might we” questions

How might we help keymakers organise their limited workspace so they can easily access key blanks, tools, and materials while working?

How might we help keymakers digitally record and retrieve essential customer and key details efficiently while minimising physical storage?

02 / MY ROLE

Research into flows.
Flows into interfaces.

I was responsible for UI/UX and app design within the team. I translated the research insights into user flows, wireframes, and interface designs tailored to roadside keymakers’ needs.

The field research and overall project were collaborative. My contribution focused on turning those shared observations into the digital experience.

My design responsibilities

  • Translate research insights into practical user flows.
  • Create wireframes to structure the app experience.
  • Develop interface designs for customer and key-related information.
  • Refine the experience using feedback from prototype testing.

03 / THE SOLUTION

Less paper.
Information in one place.

KeyMitra brings customer information and key-related records into a digital record-keeping system. The concept also supports inventory management for key blanks.

OBSERVATION

Paper takes time and space

Customer records were usually maintained manually.

DESIGN RESPONSE

Keep details together

Customer and key records live in one digital place.

INTENDED BENEFIT

Retrieve when needed

Reduce dependence on physical records and support quicker access.

Connecting the app to the work

The digital concept addresses the information-management opportunity. Inventory support helps track key blanks, while physical tool access remains a complementary workspace-design problem.

The public entry flow

The live prototype starts with a bilingual welcome action, then offers Google or email sign-in. The images above show these actual public screens. The customer-record and inventory screens require sign-in and are not reproduced here.

One tradeoff for a future review is whether visitors should be able to understand the app’s value before creating an account.

04 / TESTING & LEARNING

Try it with the people
it’s made for.

The prototype was tested with keymakers to evaluate usability and how well the interface supported their daily workflow. Feedback from those sessions informed design refinement.

Evidence level: qualitative usability testing is documented here. No numerical efficiency or adoption outcome is claimed.

Intended impact

  • Make customer-record management faster and easier.
  • Reduce dependence on paper-based records.
  • Support quicker access to customer and key information.
  • Complement the keymaker’s existing daily workflow.

These are the solution’s goals, rather than measured outcomes.

What I would document next

Bring the wireframes and record-management screens into this story, show a specific issue found during testing, and compare the interface before and after the resulting change. That would make the research-to-design connection easier to evaluate.
